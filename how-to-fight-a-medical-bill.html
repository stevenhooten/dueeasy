How to Fight a Medical Bill: The Steps in Order

Most medical bills are fought in the wrong order. Check for errors first, apply for financial assistance second, appeal or dispute third, negotiate what is left, and get every agreement in writing. The full sequence, with the deadlines that matter.

Why order matters

People usually start by calling and begging for a discount on a number that was never correct. Billing errors, missed assistance, and denied claims that can be appealed all shrink the amount before negotiation ever starts. Each step below removes a different kind of wrong charge, and skipping ahead leaves money on the table.

Step 1: Get the itemized bill and check it against your EOB

Call the provider billing office and ask for an itemized bill with CPT codes. Put it next to the explanation of benefits from your insurer. The patient responsibility line on the EOB is the amount that counts. Duplicates, services never received, and amounts above the EOB are errors, and errors are corrected, not negotiated.

Step 2: Apply for financial assistance

If the provider is a nonprofit hospital, ask for the financial assistance application before any payment plan. Free care is common at or below 200 percent of the federal poverty level, discounts often run much higher, and federal rules give you 240 days from the first bill to apply. A complete application pauses extraordinary collection actions while it is decided.

Step 3: Appeal the denial or dispute the estimate

If insurance denied the claim, appeal in writing before your plan deadline: 180 days for most private and Marketplace plans, 60 for Medicare Advantage, 120 for Original Medicare. If you were uninsured or self pay and the bill beat your Good Faith Estimate by $400 or more, a federal dispute can be opened within 120 days for a $25 fee that is credited against the amount owed if the decision lowers your bill.

Step 4: Negotiate what survives, in writing

Whatever is left after errors, assistance, and appeals is the real number. Ask for a specific reduction, 30 to 40 percent is a reasonable target, reference prompt payment, and get the agreement in writing before you pay a dollar. A verbal discount from a billing call center has a way of disappearing.

Step 5: If it lands in collections anyway

Do not pay a collector on the first call. You have 30 days from their first contact to demand validation in writing, which forces them to prove the debt and the amount. Then ask the hospital to recall the account while your assistance application is processed. Accounts get recalled more often than people expect.

Common questions

What percentage of medical bills have errors?

Estimates vary widely, and no official count exists. What is verifiable on your own bill: duplicate lines, charges for services not received, and totals above the EOB patient responsibility. Any one of those is correctable in full, which is why checking comes before negotiating.

How long do I have to fight a medical bill?

Several clocks run at once: insurer appeal deadlines run 60 to 180 days by plan type, financial assistance applications run 240 days from the first bill, Good Faith Estimate disputes run 120 days, and external review runs 4 months from a final denial. The safest move is to start the week the bill arrives.
