Negotiating a medical bill, in the right order

Check it, apply for assistance, then negotiate the rest.

The short version

Medical bills are negotiable more often than providers admit, especially after you have checked the bill for errors and applied for financial assistance. Those two steps come first, because a discount on a bill you do not owe is no discount at all. When a balance is truly yours, ask for a reduction with a specific number, and get any agreement in writing before you pay.

Rules that matter: never agree on the first call, ask for the hospital's own zero interest payment plan instead of a medical credit card, and get any agreement in writing before you pay a cent.

The order that works

  1. Check for errors first. Match the bill against your EOB and ask for an itemized bill with codes. Errors are common, and correcting one is better than discounting one.
  2. Apply for assistance second. If the hospital has a financial assistance program, a formal approval beats a phone discount and can cover the whole balance.
  3. Negotiate what is left. Ask for a lump sum settlement or a prompt pay reduction. Reductions of 30 to 40 percent are realistic targets when you can pay the reduced amount promptly.

On the call

  • Be calm, be specific, and name the exact amount you can pay.
  • Ask who has authority to approve a reduction if the first person cannot.
  • Never agree to a payment plan at the full amount while an assistance application or a correction is still open.
  • Get the agreement in writing, with the new balance and the date, before money moves.
