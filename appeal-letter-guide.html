How to appeal a denied health insurance claim

Deadlines, records, and the letter itself, in plain order.

The short version

A denial is not the final word. It is the start of an appeal process with a deadline, and the deadline depends on your plan type. Find the date on your denial letter, count your window, and file in writing with records attached. A large share of denials are overturned when the treating provider's records go in with the appeal.

If your insurer still says no after your internal appeal, you generally have the right to an external review by an independent reviewer, and the reviewer's decision binds the plan. Under the federal standard you have 4 months from the final denial notice to request it, and some states set shorter windows, so do not sit on a final denial. Your denial letter and your state department of insurance can tell you how to file. The No Surprises Help Desk is 1-800-985-3059.

What to gather before you write

  • A copy of the denial letter
  • A letter from the treating doctor explaining why the care was needed, with dates and prior treatments tried
  • Office notes, test results, and records that support the service
  • The plan page or policy section that covers this type of care, if you have it
  • For a missing authorization denial: any record of the provider requesting authorization, and whether the care was urgent

How the appeal letter should read

  1. Identify yourself, the member ID, the claim number, and the date of service.
  2. State plainly that you are appealing the denial and name the reason the plan gave.
  3. Answer that reason directly with facts and records, not frustration.
  4. Ask the plan to identify the clinical criteria it applied and the reviewer who made the decision.
  5. Close with a request for a full and fair review and a written decision.

If the final appeal still says no
